# CasaFlowAI City of San Jose Home-Extension Lifecycle Policy

## LC-1 - Lifecycle structure

CasaFlowAI organizes the synthetic home-extension project into four coordination phases:

1. **Foundation and Substructure**
   - Site preparation and excavation
   - Formwork
   - Gravel, vapor barrier, and moisture protection
   - Under-slab plumbing
   - Rebar installation
   - Concrete pour

2. **Framing and Building Envelope**
   - Floor, wall, and roof framing
   - Wall and roof sheathing
   - House wrap and roof underlayment
   - Exterior windows and doors
   - Weather-tight milestone

3. **Systems Rough-In**
   - Rough plumbing
   - Electrical rough-in
   - Mechanical/HVAC rough-in

4. **Interior and Exterior Finishes and Closeout**
   - Insulation
   - Drywall hang, tape, and finish
   - Prime and paint
   - Flooring
   - Finish plumbing and electrical
   - Trim, moldings, and interior doors
   - Exterior siding and finish
   - Final inspection readiness
   - Homeowner handoff

Inspection statuses are stored separately in the Inspection Register.

## LC-2 - Phases are not automatic hard gates

The phases organize the lifecycle but do not create an entirely linear project.

CasaFlowAI evaluates the project-specific dependency records for each activity. Activities may overlap when their own material prerequisites are satisfied and no hard permit, inspection, approved-plan, safety, privacy, or professional requirement blocks them.

An incomplete electrical rough-in may block insulation in the affected wall area without stopping independently eligible exterior work, rough plumbing, or HVAC work.

## LC-3 - Synthetic City of San Jose inspection sequence

For this project:

1. Footing/Foundation/Rebar and Underfloor/Slab inspections are separate gates; both must be `Passed - contractor confirmed` before Concrete Pour may be Ready.
2. Physical framing and sheathing enable eligible rough plumbing and HVAC work.
3. The extension must be weather-tight before Electrical Rough-In may begin.
4. Rough plumbing, electrical, and mechanical/HVAC may run in parallel after their own prerequisites.
5. Applicable Structural/Shear/Roof-nailing, Rough Plumbing, Rough Electrical, and Rough Mechanical/HVAC inspections are tracked separately.
6. Rough-frame Release follows the applicable structural and rough-trade approvals and is the final recorded gate before insulation.
7. Insulation/Energy inspection must pass before drywall.
8. Fire-rated Assembly/Drywall inspection is `Not applicable` for this synthetic permit.
9. One composite Final inspection must pass before homeowner handoff.

The synthetic permit profile determines applicability; the lifecycle never proves that an inspection passed.

## LC-4 - Homeowner-selected or purchased materials

The Decision and Selection Register records:

- Who selects the material
- Who purchases or supplies it
- Whether contractor feasibility is confirmed
- Whether the material is available when required
- Whether cost, scope, or schedule review remains pending

A homeowner selection is not by itself permission to install.

For flooring:

> Homeowner selects and purchases flooring → contractor confirms suitability and installation implications → availability is confirmed → flooring installation is evaluated against remaining dependencies.

## LC-5 - Lifecycle deviations and changes

Changed site conditions, revised openings, substitutions, and scope changes remain source-stamped proposals until properly reviewed.

CasaFlowAI preserves the original approved state, identifies affected activities, and applies the dependency, authority, and escalation policies. A phase label cannot authorize a change or clear a blocker.

## LC-6 - No independent regulatory judgment

CasaFlowAI records contractor attestations and evaluates recorded gates. It does not:

- Declare code compliance
- Independently pass an inspection
- Interpret an unclear permit or plan revision
- Replace a City inspector or qualified professional
- Assume that Final inspection creates a Certificate of Occupancy

## LC-7 - Reference basis

The synthetic sequencing profile is informed by the City of San Jose’s official Residential Construction Guidelines and Certificate of Occupancy procedures:

- https://www.sanjoseca.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/130392
- https://www.sanjoseca.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/25883/638599340560630000

These references guide the prototype’s synthetic policy. They do not replace the matched project’s permit profile or create a real regulatory determination.
